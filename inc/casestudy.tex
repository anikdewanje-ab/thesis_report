\chapter{Case Study: Storm Surge in Hamburg}
\label{ch:casestudy}

\section{Scenario Overview}
\label{sec:cs:overview}

We study evacuation coordination on one concrete case: a predicted storm surge
in Hamburg that forces the evacuation of a nursing home on the river Elbe. The
Elbe is tidal along its whole course through the city, and a storm over the
North Sea can push water up the estuary into the city centre. Risk studies for
the German Bight and the Elbe estuary model how such surges develop and which
areas they reach \parencite{oumeraci2015a}. We take this hazard as
given and do not model the flood itself. For us, its key property is the
warning time. A surge is forecast hours in advance, so responders have a
window to act, but the window is fixed. The case is therefore a planning problem
under a deadline.

The site is the Augustinum, a senior care facility at Elbchaussee 374, 22609
Hamburg, close to the river in the west of the city. Its 100 residents all have
to leave before the water arrives. Many of them cannot walk far, cannot drive,
and cannot leave on their own, so the evacuation is a transport and care
operation. Evacuation models that ignore mobility differences can underestimate
how long such an operation takes \parencite{alqahtani2025a}. The German
storm-surge work cited above studies the hazard, the risk and the damage, but
not the evacuation of vulnerable groups on the Elbe. We found no ready-made
model of this operation to reuse, which is one reason we built the case.

The case is small enough to run many times and large enough to be realistic.
The roster is fixed at two buses with 50 seats each, two school shelters with
60 beds each, one police escort and one medical team. No bus can carry all 100
residents in one trip, and no shelter can take all of them. The 120 beds leave
only 20 to spare. A plan therefore has to split the residents between the
shelters and schedule more than one run, which makes the allocation a real
decision. Bus-2 is declared a reserve, and the two systems use it differently
(Section~\ref{sec:wf:fleet}).

The case offers a large space of possible disruptions (the ones we ran never
required a change of plan, Section~\ref{sec:crit:gate0}).

\section{Organizational Agents}
\label{sec:cs:agents}

Every agent stands for a person in an organizational role, such as a
coordinator at a desk, a bus driver or a head teacher. Vehicles are passive
resources that a person drives. Each agent has a fixed identity with an invented
name, a role, a work address and an address book of the contacts it may
message. It only knows what the person in that role could see.

We gave a language model only to the agents that make a decision in the case.
ZKD relays the alarm, books the reports and countersigns a stand-down. The
schools report their free beds and confirm arrivals, and the nursing home
reports its residents and when the building is empty. None of the four has to
choose between options, so all four are scripted.

The workflow baseline keeps this roster, but one scripted Incident Commander (IC)
replaces the two coordinating agents, BIS and Hochbahn. The other agents are
not started in a baseline run. An execution model carries out the IC's orders
in their place, so no decision on the baseline side comes from a model.
Chapter~\ref{ch:agentic} describes how the agents reason, and
Chapter~\ref{ch:workflow} describes the baseline.

\section{Coordination Storyline}
\label{sec:cs:storyline}

The mission follows ten steps (Figure~\ref{fig:cs:storyline}). They fix what
has to happen and who is responsible, and leave the decisions to the
coordinator.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Identification.} ZKD relays the flood alert to BIS.
  \item \textbf{Evacuation assessment.} The Augustinum confirms 100 residents
  and the deadline.
  \item \textbf{Resource allocation.} Each school offers 60 beds, and BIS
  decides the split.
  \item \textbf{Route planning.} Each driver routes its own legs with
  OpenRouteService.\footnote{\url{https://openrouteservice.org}}
  \item \textbf{Logistics.} Hochbahn sends BIS a run schedule, by default two
  runs of bus-1 with bus-2 in reserve.
  \item \textbf{Police coordination.} The patrol car leads the convoy on every
  run.
  \item \textbf{Medical support.} The ambulance follows at the tail on every run.
  \item \textbf{Evacuation execution.} The driver reports each departure, and
  the home reports once whether the building is cleared.
  \item \textbf{Arrival and handover.} The school confirms the intake, and
  Hochbahn tells BIS the run is complete.
  \item \textbf{Mission report.} BIS reports to ZKD, which closes the record.
\end{enumerate}

Steps 8 and 9 repeat for every bus run. How many residents go to each school,
and on which run, is the coordinator's decision. The baseline's commander walks
the same steps in four phases (Table~\ref{tab:wf:phases}).

One further path lies outside the ten steps. If BIS finds that no recovery is
possible, ZKD countersigns a stand-down and BIS recalls the transport and the
escorts. No evaluated scenario triggered it.

\section{Disruption Scenarios}
\label{sec:cs:disruptions}

Every run begins with the alarm and ends at the forecast high water. In all
scenarios the flood reaches the zone at 12:00, and a resident who reaches a
shelter later does not count as rescued. We evaluated seven scenarios in two
arms (Table~\ref{tab:cs:scenarios}). The two \emph{anticipated} scenarios were
known while the systems were built. S01 has no disruption, and the baseline
has a written branch for the road closure in S02. The five \emph{novel} scenarios were supplied by the domain expert,
Rüdiger Höfert, from a brief that named only the kind of disruption wanted.
They were revealed after the baseline was frozen on 2026-09-10
(Section~\ref{sec:eval:firewall}). A shelter closure (S03) had no agentic
counterpart and was not scored. Section~\ref{sec:eval:heldout} lists the
response each scenario expects.

\section{Assumptions and Simplifications}
\label{sec:cs:assumptions}

The case makes the following simplifications, for both systems.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Residents are a count,} with no behaviour and no subgroups by
  mobility or care level.
  \item \textbf{The flood is a deadline.} Each zone has one arrival time, and
  the surge is not simulated.
  \item \textbf{Roads have no traffic.} Distances and times come from
  OpenRouteService, with a straight-line fallback.
  \item \textbf{Bus seats are not enforced.} Shelter beds and the deadline are,
  and residents above a shelter's beds do not count as safe.
  \item \textbf{Messages always arrive,} after a fixed delay.
  \item \textbf{One clock speed.} All runs use 8.4 simulated seconds per real
  second.
  \item \textbf{One case.} There is one facility, with two buses and two
  shelters.
\end{itemize}

These choices keep the case small enough to run hundreds of times, and they
limit what the results can show (Chapters~\ref{ch:critique}
and~\ref{ch:discussion}).
